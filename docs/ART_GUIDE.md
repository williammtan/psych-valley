# Project Psyche — Art Production Guide

**Read this completely before writing a single pixel.** Every art module must obey it.
It exists so that eleven different builders produce work that looks like one artist made it.

---

## 1. The pipeline

All art is *generated by TypeScript code* in `tools/art/assets/*.ts`. There are no
hand-drawn PNGs, no downloaded assets, and no reference-game assets. Ever.

```
tools/art/lib/pixel.ts      Surface + drawing primitives (your only drawing API)
tools/art/lib/palette.ts    THE palette. Do not invent colours outside it.
tools/art/lib/autotile.ts   blob autotiling for terrain-like materials
tools/art/lib/registry.ts   ArtBuild: addTile / add / addStrip / addAnim
tools/art/assets/*.ts       one module per art domain — your file
tools/art/build.ts          packs everything; writes public/assets + art_preview/
```

Build with `npm run art`. It writes:
- `public/assets/tiles.png` — 16×16 tileset (extruded, margin 1, spacing 2)
- `public/assets/atlas.png` + `atlas.json` — every sprite
- `public/assets/art.json` — name→index tables, blob tables, animation defs
- `art_preview/sprite_*.png` and `art_preview/tiles_all.png` — **4× inspection sheets.
  Look at these with the Read tool after every build. This is not optional.**

### Naming

| kind | pattern | example |
|---|---|---|
| tile | `tile/<area>/<family>_<variant>` | `tile/town/grass_3` |
| blob set | `blob/<material>` (via `registerBlobSet`) | `blob/path` |
| prop | `prop/<area>/<name>` | `prop/town/barrel` |
| character frame | `char/<who>/<anim>_<dir>_<n>` | `char/sera/walk_s_2` |
| enemy frame | `enemy/<who>/<anim>_<dir>_<n>` | `enemy/bramble/walk_s_1` |
| fx | `fx/<name>_<n>` | `fx/dust_2` |
| ui | `ui/<name>` | `ui/panel_9slice` |

A "family" is the name with the trailing `_<digits>` stripped. The runtime picks a
random-but-stable variant from a family per world position, so **always ship 3–6
variants of anything that tiles across an area.** One variant = visible repetition
= instantly reads as a prototype.

Animations: `b.addStrip('char/mira/idle_s', frames, { key: 'mira_idle_s', frameRate: 4, repeat: -1 })`.

## 2. Scale and perspective

- Tile: **16×16 px**. Camera shows 30×17 tiles (480×270 internal, 4× on a 1080p screen).
- Perspective: **top-down three-quarter.** You see the top of the ground and the
  *front face* of anything vertical. A barrel shows its lid as a squashed ellipse
  and its body below it. Never draw pure orthographic top-down.
- Characters: **24 wide × 32 tall canvas, feet on the bottom row.** Figure occupies
  roughly 28 px of height. Head is ~10 px tall — deliberately large, so faces read.
- Props are anchored **bottom-centre** on their tile. A 2-tile-wide prop is a 32 px
  wide sprite, not two 16 px sprites.
- Buildings are single large sprites (e.g. 96×112), anchored bottom-centre, with
  their doorway centred on a tile boundary.

---

## 3. The rules that make it cohere

1. **Light comes from the upper-left.** Top and left surfaces catch `ramp[3]`/`ramp[4]`;
   bottom and right go to `ramp[1]`/`ramp[0]`. This is never negotiable.
2. **No pure black, no pure white.** Outlines use `P.OUTLINE` (#241d33) or
   `P.OUTLINE_WARM`. Brightest highlight in town is `#fff0a8`-ish, never `#ffffff`
   except tiny specular sparks and foam.
3. **Shadows drift violet, highlights drift warm.** That single hue rule is most of
   what makes the palette feel authored.
4. **Every sprite gets a contact shadow** — a squashed dark ellipse at its base,
   `P.OUTLINE` at ~30% alpha. Without it, things look like stickers.
5. **Outline selectively, not uniformly.** Characters and enemies get a full 1 px
   `P.OUTLINE` silhouette so they pop against terrain. Environment props get a dark
   outline only on their *lower and right* edges; a full outline makes scenery
   look like clip-art.
6. **Two ramp steps minimum on any flat area.** A single flat colour anywhere
   larger than 4×4 px reads as unfinished. Use mottling, dithering, or texture
   marks — but keep contrast low (see the grass note in `terrain.ts`).
7. **Read at 4×, judge at 1×.** Detail that vanishes at 1× is wasted; noise that
   only appears at 4× is harmless. Check the inspection sheet *and* the in-game
   screenshot.
8. **Silhouette first.** Cover the colours: can you still tell what it is, and tell
   two NPCs apart? If not, fix the shape before touching the shading.

## 4. Colour discipline

Use `import * as P from '../lib/palette.js'` and take colours from the ramps.
If you truly need a colour that does not exist, **add it to `palette.ts` as a named
ramp** rather than inlining a hex literal — one file must remain the source of truth.

Hue reservations — do not violate these, they carry gameplay meaning:

| hue | reserved for |
|---|---|
| saturated violet `ECHO_VIOLET` / `#a681e6` | The Echo and everything it has touched |
| luminous cyan `ECHO_CYAN` / `#9de8ff` | Echo runes, active puzzle state, Mote |
| warm amber `LANTERN` / `WINDOW_AMBER` | safety, home, lit windows, festival lanterns |
| `FLOWER_*` | decoration only — never on gameplay-critical objects |

Town never uses full-saturation violet or cyan. That's why anomalies read instantly.

## 5. Density

From the reference pack: a scene needs three layers or it looks like a prototype.

1. **Gameplay layer** — paths, doors, NPCs, puzzle objects, enemies.
2. **Structural layer** — buildings, walls, water, trees, fences, cliffs.
3. **Texture layer** — flowers, grass tufts, pebbles, crates, signage, laundry,
   crates, hanging herbs, shadows, particles.

Your module must supply enough *texture layer* variety that a map author can fill
space without repeating themselves. Concretely: if you build props for an area,
ship **at least 15–25 distinct props** for it, in a mix of sizes (1×1, 1×2, 2×1,
2×2), not five props at four sizes.

## 6. Animation

- Idle: 2–4 frames at 3–4 fps. A 1 px vertical bob of the head/torso is enough.
  Everything alive must breathe. Static NPCs kill a town.
- Walk: 6 frames at 10 fps (contact, down, pass, contact, down, pass) with a 1 px
  body bob on the pass frames.
- Attack: 3 frames — wind-up (2 frames held short), strike, recover.
- Environment: banners/foliage sway via the runtime's `sway` on `ObjectSpec`;
  you don't need to animate those by hand.
- Water, fire, lanterns, glowing runes: 3–4 frame loops, generated as variants.

---

## 7. Definition of done for an art module

- [ ] `npm run art` succeeds and prints your module's tile/sprite counts
- [ ] you have **looked at** `art_preview/sprite_<yourmodule>*.png` with the Read tool
- [ ] silhouettes are distinct; nothing is a recoloured duplicate of its neighbour
- [ ] every family that tiles has ≥3 variants
- [ ] every sprite has a contact shadow or sits on one
- [ ] light is upper-left everywhere
- [ ] no colour outside `palette.ts`
- [ ] nothing resembles a specific Stardew Valley or Zelda asset

---

## 8. Explicitly forbidden

- Copying, tracing, or recolouring anything from `project_psyche_reference_pack/`.
- Loading any external asset, font, or texture at runtime.
- Inlining hex colours instead of extending `palette.ts`.
- Shipping one variant of a tiling texture.
- Emoji, DOM/HTML elements, or vector graphics as a substitute for pixel art.
